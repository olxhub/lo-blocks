## METADATA
**Title**: Examining  School  Funding  Inequity  and  Impact  on  African  American  High  School  Dropouts
**Author**: Michelle  Janette  Williams
**Dissertation Institution**: Walden  University **Publication Year**: 2023

## Abstract:
This  quantitative  study  examines  the  association  between  school  funding  inequity  and  the  dropout  rates  of  African  American  students. While  previous  research  has  explored  factors  such  as  peer  pressure  and  socioeconomic  status,  this  study  specifically  focuses  on  how  inadequate  school  funding  impacts  dropout  rates. Using  equity  theory  as  the  theoretical  framework,  the  study  analyzes  publicly  available  archival  data  from  2015-2020  from  the  State  of  Delaware's  Department  of  Education. A  Pearson  correlation  analysis  conducted  through  SPSS  identified  a  relationship  between  lower  school  funding  and  higher  dropout  rates  among  African  American  students  in  the  Red  Clay  School  District. The  findings  suggest  that  inadequate  school  funding  contributes  to  dropout  rates,  reinforcing  the  need  for  policy  reforms  to  improve  resource  allocation  in  high-poverty  schools.

## Introduction:
Educational  inequity  remains  a  persistent  issue,  disproportionately  affecting  low-income  and  minority  students. While  policies  such  as  Title  I  and  the  Every  Student  Succeeds  Act  (ESSA)  have  attempted  to  close  funding  gaps,  disparities  continue  to  exist. African  American  students  are  particularly  vulnerable  to  resource  inequities,  leading  to  higher  dropout  rates  and  long-term  socioeconomic  disadvantages.

## Background:
School  funding  in  the  U. S. is  primarily  determined  by  local  property  taxes,  leading  to  significant  disparities  in  educational  resources. Research  suggests  that  African  American  students  in  underfunded  schools  face  increased  dropout  risks  due  to  fewer  academic  and  support  services. The  study  explores  whether  the  timing  of  dropout  (9th-12th  grade)  correlates  with  school  funding  levels  in  the  Red  Clay  School  District  in  Delaware.

## Method:
### Study  Design:
Quantitative  correlational  analysis

### Data  Source:
Publicly  available  archival  data  from  2015-2020  (Delaware  Department  of  Education)

### Variables:
#### Independent  Variable:
Per-pupil  school  funding

#### Dependent  Variable:
Grade  level  at  which  African  American  students  dropped  out  (9th-12th  grade)

#### Statistical  Analysis:
Pearson  correlation  in  SPSS  to  measure  the  association  between  school  funding  levels  and  dropout  rates

## Results:
The  study  identified  a  significant  correlation  between  lower  school  funding  and  higher  dropout  rates. African  American  students  in  lower-funded  schools  were  more  likely  to  drop  out  earlier  (9th-10th  grade)  compared  to  students  in  better-funded  schools.

The  equity  theory  framework  suggests  that  students  in  underfunded  schools  experience  resource  inequities  that  discourage  persistence  in  education.

## Discussion:
The  findings  reinforce  existing  research  on  educational  inequity  and  suggest  that  higher  per-pupil  funding  is  associated  with  improved  student  retention. The  study  highlights  the  need  for  funding  reforms,  such  as  weighted  student  funding  models,  to  better  allocate  resources  to  high-need  schools.

## Conclusion:
School  funding  inequities  contribute  to  higher  dropout  rates  among  African  American  students. Addressing  these  disparities  through  policy  changes  and  increased  investment  in  low-income  schools  could  improve  educational  outcomes  and  reduce  dropout  rates. The  study  provides  empirical  evidence  to  support  advocacy  efforts  for  school  finance  reform.
