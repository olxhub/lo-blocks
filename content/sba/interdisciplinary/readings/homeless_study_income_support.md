## Income Support Policies and the Rise of Student and Family Homelessness

**Author:** Zachary Parolin
**Journal:** The ANNALS of the American Academy of Political and Social Science (2021), 693(1), 46–63
**DOI:** 10.1177/0002716220981847

**Abstract (Summary):**
This study examines whether the generosity and accessibility of publicly provided income support policies influence levels of family and student homelessness in the United States. Using administrative data on student homelessness across most U.S. public school districts and state-level variation in access to cash and near-cash assistance programs, the analysis assesses how differences in policy generosity are associated with homelessness rates. Findings indicate that greater access to TANF cash assistance is significantly associated with lower rates of family homelessness, particularly in districts with majority–Black or Native American student populations. Evidence for the effects of SNAP and EITC is inconclusive.

**Introduction:**
Family homelessness has increased in the U.S. in recent years, as indicated by Department of Education data showing rising numbers of students experiencing homelessness. Traditional explanations emphasize housing costs and structural economic conditions, but this study focuses on how variation in income support policy design — specifically cash and near-cash assistance accessibility — may shape family homelessness outcomes.

**Background:**
The U.S. welfare state includes multiple income support programs intended to raise economic resources for low-income families. Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF) provides direct cash assistance, whereas the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) offers food support, and the Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC) provides wage subsidies for certain working families. Prior research suggests that income support reduces economic hardship, but less is known about how variation in policy generosity and access affects homelessness specifically.

**Method:**
The analysis combines administrative data on student homelessness from public school districts with measures of state-level income support policy generosity and accessibility. Focus is placed on three major programs: TANF, SNAP, and the EITC. Using cross-state and over-time differences in program access and generosity, the study estimates how these variations relate to differences in homelessness rates among students and families with children.

**Results:**
Greater access to cash assistance through TANF is associated with significantly lower rates of family homelessness. Increases in the accessibility of TANF cash benefits correspond with measurable declines in family homelessness rates. The negative association between TANF access and homelessness is particularly pronounced in majority–Black and Native American school districts. In contrast, greater access to SNAP benefits does not show a consistent relationship with reduced homelessness, and increased generosity of the EITC yields inconclusive effects on homelessness levels.

**Discussion:**
These findings suggest that direct cash assistance may play a distinctive role in reducing family homelessness, beyond what in-kind (SNAP) or conditional wage subsidy (EITC) programs achieve. The study highlights how policy retrenchment — particularly declines in TANF accessibility — may help explain recent trends in homelessness among families with children. It also underscores the importance of considering racial and ethnic disparities in program effects.

**Conclusion:**
Family and student homelessness in the U.S. is associated with the accessibility of income support policies. Greater access to cash assistance through TANF is linked to lower homelessness rates, suggesting that policy design within the welfare state influences housing stability. SNAP and EITC do not show clear protective associations in this study. Integrating income support policy into homelessness research provides a broader understanding of structural determinants of housing insecurity.
