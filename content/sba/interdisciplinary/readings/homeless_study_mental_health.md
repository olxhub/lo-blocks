## Prevalence of Mental Health Disorders Among Individuals Experiencing Homelessness: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

**Authors:** Rebecca Barry, Jennifer Anderson, Lan Tran, Anees Bahji, Gina Dimitropoulos, S. Monty Ghosh, Julia Kirkham, Geoffrey Messier, Scott B. Patten, Katherine Rittenbach, & Dallas Seitz
**Journal:** JAMA Psychiatry (2024), 81(7), 691–699
**DOI:** 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2024.0426

**Abstract (Summary):**
This systematic review and meta-analysis examines the prevalence of mental health disorders among individuals experiencing homelessness. Synthesizing evidence from 85 studies involving 48,414 participants, the analysis found that mental health disorders are highly prevalent among homeless populations. Approximately 67% of individuals experiencing homelessness met criteria for a current mental health disorder, while 77% met criteria for a lifetime mental health disorder. Substance use disorders were the most common conditions identified, followed by antisocial personality disorder, major depression, bipolar disorder, and schizophrenia. Findings suggest that homelessness and mental illness are closely connected and that homelessness responses should incorporate coordinated mental health and social support services.

**Introduction:**
Homelessness is frequently associated with mental illness, yet estimates of psychiatric disorder prevalence among homeless populations have varied across studies and settings. Understanding the extent of mental health needs among people experiencing homelessness is important for developing effective housing, treatment, and support systems. This study synthesizes evidence on the prevalence of mental health disorders among homeless populations through systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Background:**
Previous research has consistently documented elevated rates of psychiatric disorders among people experiencing homelessness, including substance use disorders, depression, schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, and personality disorders. Mental illness may interact with homelessness through multiple pathways involving trauma, social isolation, unemployment, housing instability, and reduced access to treatment. However, prevalence estimates across studies have differed substantially, creating the need for a comprehensive synthesis of findings.

**Method:**
The study conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of research examining mental health disorders among individuals experiencing homelessness. Eighty-five studies involving 48,414 participants were included. Studies were identified through systematic database searches and screened using established inclusion criteria. Statistical meta-analysis techniques were used to estimate pooled prevalence rates for current and lifetime mental health disorders across homeless populations.

**Results:**
The analysis found very high rates of psychiatric disorders among people experiencing homelessness:

- 67% met criteria for a current mental health disorder.
- 77% met criteria for a lifetime mental health disorder.
- Lifetime prevalence rates were higher among men (86%) than women (69%).

Specific disorder prevalence estimates included:

- Substance use disorders: 44%
- Antisocial personality disorder: 26%
- Major depression: 19%
- Bipolar disorder: 8%
- Schizophrenia: 7%

The findings demonstrate that mental health disorders are substantially more prevalent among homeless populations than in the general population.

**Discussion:**
The findings suggest that mental health disorders represent a major concern among people experiencing homelessness. High prevalence rates across multiple psychiatric conditions indicate that homelessness and mental illness are deeply interconnected. Substance use disorders and severe mental illnesses may contribute to housing instability, while homelessness itself may worsen psychiatric symptoms through chronic stress, trauma exposure, and social exclusion. The authors argue that homelessness interventions should integrate housing support with mental health and substance use treatment services.

**Conclusion:**
Mental health disorders are highly prevalent among individuals experiencing homelessness, particularly substance use disorders and severe psychiatric conditions. The strong association between homelessness and mental illness highlights the need for coordinated approaches that combine stable housing, psychiatric treatment, substance use services, and broader social supports.
